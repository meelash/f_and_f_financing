Open source · Self-hosted · Interest-free

Buy a home with family and friends, then buy them out over time.

Friends & Family Financing is a small web app that keeps the books for a shared purchase. Everyone chips in to buy the item. The person using it pays rent to the others and gradually buys their shares, until they own it outright. No bank and no interest. Everyone can see the same ledger.

The premise

A way to finance a big purchase, usually a home, through people you trust instead of a mortgage. It's a co-ownership arrangement, not a loan.

01 · Buy together

Everyone owns a share

The occupant and one or more family members or friends buy the item together. Ownership is split pro rata to what each person put in, or by any other split you all agree to.

02 · Pay rent

The user pays fair rent

The person living in the home (the occupant) pays an agreed monthly rent. Each owner receives their share of it according to what they own. The occupant's own share of the rent stays with them.

03 · Buy equity

Buy shares over time

Anything the occupant pays beyond the other owners' rent buys shares from them, pro rata. This includes their own share of the rent, extra payments and lump sums. As their ownership grows, the rent they owe to others shrinks.

04 · Own it

Until they own 100%

The process continues until the occupant has bought every share. The app projects when that will happen at any monthly payment you choose.

Why it's permissible

  • No interest, anywhere. Nobody lends money and nobody is owed a fixed return. The investors own real shares of a real asset.
  • Rent is for use. The occupant pays rent for living in the portion of the home they don't own. Each owner's income comes from their own share.
  • Shares are sold at a price agreed at the time. The valuation used for equity purchases is agreed by all parties when the shares are bought. It is not fixed in advance.
  • Shared risk. If the home gains or loses value, each owner gains or loses in proportion to their share.

The arrangement involves no interest and is designed to be 100% permissible.

What the app does for you

The arrangement is simple, but tracking it for years is not. Rent splits change every month as ownership shifts. Property taxes get prepaid and reimbursed, payments come in late or in pieces, and at some point someone asks, "so how much do I own now?"

This app records every payment, splits it correctly and moves ownership from month to month. Everyone logs in and sees the same ledger, so nobody needs a spreadsheet and nobody argues about the numbers.

A worked example

Yusuf wants to buy a $400,000 home. He has $100,000; his uncle Idris puts in $300,000. They agree on rent of $2,000/month. Yusuf also prepaid the year's $4,800 property tax, so $400/month of rent goes back to him as reimbursement. (All the screenshots below use this made-up scenario.)

Month 1, step by step

Rent$2,000.00
− Tax reimbursement to Yusuf$400.00
Net rent split by ownership$1,600.00
Idris's dividend (75%): paid in cash$1,200.00
Yusuf's dividend (25%) + his $400 reimbursement$800.00
Used to buy shares from Idris at $400k+0.20%

Yusuf sends Idris the full $2,000. $1,200 is Idris's rent and $800 buys shares. If he prefers, Yusuf can tick "take in cash" and keep his dividend or reimbursement instead.

Idris's rent income goes down a little every month because he owns less of the house. More of each payment therefore goes toward Yusuf's equity.

Projection: paying $3,000/month from here, Yusuf owns the home outright in about 9.7 years.

Features

Screenshots from a live instance using the example above.

Guided first-time setup

Enter the property, its agreed starting valuation, the rent, the tax arrangement and what each person contributed. Ownership percentages are calculated pro rata from the contributions. Tick a box to set them manually if you agreed on a different split.

The setup creates a login for each person, and they must change their temporary password the first time they sign in.

Record a payment and preview the split

Enter what was paid and see exactly where every dollar goes before anything is saved:

  • Each investor's dividend, the occupant's dividend and any tax reimbursement
  • How much equity is bought and everyone's ownership before and after
  • How much cash to actually send to the investors

Payments can be entered as a total or as cash sent. Late or early payments and several installments for the same month are all handled.

Taxes and home expenses

If the occupant prepays property tax or pays for a repair, record it once. The app then suggests a reimbursement on each rent payment until the amount is paid back.

Alternatively, set up a tax reserve that collects a fixed amount each month and pays the bill when it's due.

A complete, shared ledger

Every rent payment and tax payment is listed with its dividends, equity purchase and reimbursement. Every entry is written to an audit log, and the whole ledger can be exported to CSV.

Ownership timeline

See who owned what, and what it was worth, after every transaction. This answers "how much do I own now?" and "how much did I own last March?"

Buyout projection

Pick a monthly payment and see how long until the occupant owns 100%, with a month-by-month table of dividends, equity bought and total rent paid. Export it as CSV or as a PDF to share.

Settings: rent, valuation and taxes

When everyone agrees on a new rent or a new valuation, update it here. The change applies from the current month on, and later equity purchases are priced at the new valuation. Admins can also record tax payments and reset a partner's password.

What investors see

Investors get their own login and see the same ledger, ownership timeline and projections. They can check the numbers whenever they like without asking.

Current limitations

One occupant, and the setup wizard supports one investor. The accounting engine can split rent and equity purchases across several investors pro rata. However, the setup wizard only creates a single occupant and a single investor, and there is no screen yet for adding more investors. Each instance also tracks one partnership at a time.

If you'd like multi-investor or multi-occupant support, please reach out to the developer by opening an issue. Hearing that people need it is the best way to get it built.

This software keeps records. It isn't legal, tax or religious advice. Put your arrangement in a written agreement, and check how co-ownership and rental income are treated where you live.

Deploy your own

It's a standard Next.js app with a PostgreSQL database. The only required setting is DATABASE_URL. Each family runs its own private copy, so your financial data stays with you.

Easiest: Vercel + Neon (both have free tiers)

  1. Fork the repository to your GitHub account.
  2. Create a Postgres database. A free Neon project works well; so does Supabase, Railway or any other Postgres 14+. Copy its connection string.
  3. Create the tables from your computer (needs Node.js 20+): 
    git clone https://github.com/<you>/f_and_f_financing.git
cd f_and_f_financing
npm install
DATABASE_URL="postgresql://…your connection string…" npx prisma migrate deploy
  4. Import the fork into Vercel and add one environment variable: DATABASE_URL = your connection string. Click Deploy.
  5. Open https://your-app.vercel.app/setup straight away and run the setup wizard. This creates the partnership and the logins.
Until the first partnership exists, anyone with the URL can open the setup page. Run it right after deploying. Once a partnership exists, only an admin can create another.
Self-host with Docker

The repo includes a production Dockerfile (Next.js standalone output, runs as a non-root user). Point it at any Postgres database:

docker build -t ff-financing .

# create the tables once (from a checkout with npm install done)
DATABASE_URL="postgresql://user:pass@host:5432/ffp" npx prisma migrate deploy

docker run -d -p 3000:3000 \
  -e DATABASE_URL="postgresql://user:pass@host:5432/ffp" \
  ff-financing

Put it behind HTTPS (for example Caddy or a Cloudflare Tunnel). In production, session cookies are sent over HTTPS only.

Try it locally 
git clone https://github.com/meelash/f_and_f_financing.git
cd f_and_f_financing
npm install
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d          # local Postgres on :5432
npx prisma migrate deploy
npm run dev

Then open http://localhost:3000/setup and create a partnership.

Under the hood
  • Next.js (App Router), React, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS
  • PostgreSQL via Prisma, with money and ownership stored as exact decimals
  • scrypt password hashing, httpOnly session cookies, login rate limiting and security headers
  • Audit log with before/after snapshots for every change
  • The accounting engine is a pure, unit-tested module (src/lib/accounting/monthly-payment.ts). Projections run the same engine forward month by month.
